# People, groups, and fashion

Use for human subjects, clothing, portraits, and figure-led scenes. Load media guidance separately. Human detail should match framing and expression, not impose photographic anatomy on every style.

## Decisions

Identify the relevant identity anchors, age as specified, body proportions, expression, gaze, crop, pose, and relationship to other subjects. Do not infer biographical identity from an image. Preserve meaningful differences rather than converging on an idealized face or body.

For poses, resolve support and weight before fine gesture: feet or seat contact, pelvis orientation, ribcage rotation, shoulder position, head turn/tilt, then limbs. Distinguish the subject's left/right from the viewer's. Use exact angles only when provided or genuinely clarifying; invented anatomical precision can obscure a readable pose.

For groups, establish who interacts with whom, spacing, overlap, scale, and gaze. Avoid treating a crowd as repeated identical bodies. A figure looking at an object must have a plausible line of sight unless deliberate impossibility is part of the work.

## Visible description

Describe expression through a few mutually compatible signals: eye focus, brow tension, mouth, jaw, head position. Do not stack conflicting emotions on every facial feature. Let context carry subtle feelings.

Hands and feet need scale, orientation, contact, and function where visible. Check hand-to-face/body scale, palm-to-finger proportions, consistent sizes across both hands, and how fingers grip or rest. Do not add 'slender' or 'delicate' by habit when neutral hands are intended. Check ankle/foot orientation and support; hidden anatomy need not be exhaustively prescribed.

For clothing, resolve silhouette, panels, layering, openings, closures, attachment, and coverage before ornament. Consider fabric weight, stiffness, transparency, stretch, and how gravity/contact produce folds. Embroidery belongs to a surface and has a visible motif scale, distribution, density, and edge treatment. Distinguish printed motifs, woven pattern, stitching, and raised ornament when relevant.

Describe craft through decisions such as controlled seam placement, relief, spacing, hems, trim, and transitions, rather than repeated 'luxury' claims. Accessories must be requested or justified, not automatically added as prestige signals.

## Conflicts and review

Check balance, disconnected or impossible contacts, contradictory torso/head directions, unclear overlapping limbs, and garments that require incompatible support. Do not preserve shadows that must change with clothing. Reconcile full-body framing with close-up-level detail. Do not add an extra detail panel without consent.

Assess silhouette, expression, fabric behavior, and identity before microtexture. For stylized work, preserve deliberate proportion rules instead of forcing realism. Keep coverage and styling faithful to this task rather than importing past preferences.

## Ask or research when

Ask about competing identity/pose references or a consequential silhouette choice. Research named historical garments or uncertain specialist construction when fidelity matters. Otherwise use observable descriptions rather than inventing authoritative craft names.
