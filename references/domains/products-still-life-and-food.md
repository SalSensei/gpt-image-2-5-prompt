# Products, still life, and food

## Decisions

Determine whether recognition, appetite, material study, narrative symbolism, or formal arrangement leads. Identify the hero object, exact count, required visible faces, label priority, arrangement, surface, and crop. For a recognizable product, establish geometry and distinctive components before styling.

## Visible description

Differentiate structure from finish: seams, apertures, bevels, lid alignment, wall thickness, transparent contents, and printed marks. Assign roughness, reflectivity, and translucency by material. Metallic highlights should describe form; glass should remain distinguishable from its background. A white object on white may need subtle value separation, not an automatic black outline.

Arrange overlaps and spacing so the important silhouette or label remains visible. Connect objects to their supporting surface through appropriate contact. Reflections should relate to the scene's illumination and environment; avoid asking for a glossy object with all reflections removed unless a deliberately flattened treatment is intended.

For food, specify preparation state and cut surfaces before styling: crumb, layered structure, glaze, skin, moisture, or melt. Steam, condensation, droplets, and crumbs must fit temperature and intent rather than appearing as an appetite bundle. Match utensils, serving scale, gravity, and support to the food.

## Conflicts and review

Check product shape and part count, repeated or misspelled labels, unsupported floating pieces, incompatible material cues, and garnish obscuring the food. Tiny print cannot be made readable merely by saying it is exact. Consider the intended view size and simplify the layout or ask which copy has priority.

A commercial pack shot may favor clarity; a still life may favor ambiguity. Do not impose the same evenly lit e-commerce treatment on both. Do not beautify away marks that establish the required product identity.

## Ask or research when

Ask for exact label copy or which reference determines geometry when ambiguous. Verify claims about technical internals, material composition, ingredients, or preparation when factual accuracy matters. Never invent a product specification or promotional claim to fill a design gap.
