# Patterns, ornament, and applied surfaces

## Decisions

Distinguish a repeat tile, a larger pattern field, a border, an engineered placement print, and a mockup on an object. Establish motif family, scale, spacing, orientation, density, palette, and application. A garment print may have placement constraints that a wallpaper repeat does not.

## Visible description

For repeats, define straight, offset, mirrored, or other intended structure when needed. Motifs crossing an edge must continue on the opposite edge for a seamless tile. Keep repeat rhythm coherent without making every element mechanically identical unless desired.

Organize figure-ground balance, secondary fillers, directional bias, and empty intervals. Use motif scale relative to the eventual surface; a small screen preview may hide how large a repeat feels on a wall. Distinguish printed pattern from woven or embroidered structure.

For applied mockups, account for curvature, seams, folds, cropping, and perspective. Do not distort the underlying product merely to display the whole pattern. A border or placement motif has a deliberate relationship to edges and landmarks.

## Conflicts and review

Check visible seams, clipped motifs, accidental rows, inconsistent spacing, disruptive color clusters, and orientation changes. Prompting for seamlessness is not proof: inspect the result in a tiled arrangement before claiming continuity. This skill can describe that review need without silently editing source images.

Do not confuse a single finished pattern image with multiple distinct concept alternatives. Do not promise manufacturing repeats, embroidery programs, or exact color separations from raster generation.

## Ask or research when

Ask for application and repeat versus placement when the distinction changes composition. Research historically specific ornament or craft structure when accuracy matters. Avoid inventing cultural meanings for familiar motifs.
