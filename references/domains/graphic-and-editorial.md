# Graphic, typographic, and editorial work

## Decisions

Identify the actual deliverable and viewing context: poster, cover, editorial page, packaging face, identity concept, or photographed printed object. Separate information hierarchy from decorative language. Determine which copy is mandatory, its reading order, and whether typography or imagery is the subject.

## Visible description

Specify the relationship of headline, supporting copy, imagery, margins, and alignment. A grid can establish order or provide a structure for intentional disruption. Use scale and spacing to communicate hierarchy rather than adding more type styles.

Describe letterform character through proportion, weight, width, contrast, terminals, or rhythm where helpful. Exact font names can be cues but do not guarantee a licensed font or faithful glyph reproduction. Preserve supplied wording and language. Specify line breaks or appearance count when consequential.

Consider type as shape: a large word may anchor a composition, crop at an edge, or occupy an object-like area. Make intentional illegibility explicit; otherwise retain the copy's practical reading function.

Distinguish flat artwork from a mockup on paper or packaging. Ink effects belong to a chosen process. A logo concept should work through silhouette and spacing; raster output does not become a vector master or an approved trademark by description.

## Conflicts and review

Check hierarchy at intended size, cramped copy, inconsistent alignments, text hidden behind imagery, and motifs that overwhelm the message. Do not add invented taglines or logos to a text-free request. Avoid generic 'no logos' when a brand mark is required.

A restrained poster may need fewer elements; maximalist editorial work may need carefully organized abundance. Do not impose a universal white background, minimalism, or no-gradient rule.

## Ask or research when

Ask for required copy, its priority, or permission to simplify excessive content. Verify an unfamiliar script or historical typographic treatment when correctness matters. Do not promise print-ready separations, font files, bleed accuracy, or editable layouts from an image alone.
