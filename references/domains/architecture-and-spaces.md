# Architecture, interiors, and landscape design

## Decisions

Resolve viewpoint and eye level, extent of space, focal destination, circulation, human scale, depth, and the relationship between interior and exterior. Determine whether the image is a plausible place, conceptual proposal, miniature, or deliberately impossible architecture.

## Visible description

Use spatial relationships: doorway opening into a courtyard, foreground seating aligned with a window, roof supported by visible structure. Choose meaningful scale references rather than arbitrary dimensions. Coordinate horizon, verticals, and perspective cues for the selected medium.

Distinguish structural members, cladding, joints, edge thickness, openings, thresholds, and furnishings. Describe weathering or use traces only where they support the brief. Material changes should meet intelligibly; do not distribute stone, timber, brass, and glass as an unconnected luxury inventory.

Let daylight enter through identified openings and consider how it reaches surfaces. Artificial light can reveal paths or zones without lighting every surface equally. Landscape design includes planting masses, ground levels, paths, water edges, and seasonal condition, not just foliage as decoration.

## Conflicts and review

Check blocked circulation, mismatched furniture scale, unstable supports in a realistic proposal, duplicate openings, impossible room connections, and conflicting light directions. If strict geometry is required, an image prompt is a concept tool rather than construction documentation.

A watercolor may compress detail or simplify perspective intentionally. An interior photograph and an architectural elevation should not share the same spatial instructions. A requested view through a wall may be a section or cutaway; establish that representation rather than silently making walls transparent.

## Ask or research when

Ask when the view cannot show all requested spaces or dimensions conflict. Research named building traditions, uncertain structural systems, or historically specific joinery when fidelity matters. Do not invent engineering compliance or make a generated space sound buildable without evidence.
