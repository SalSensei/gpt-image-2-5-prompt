# Abstract and nonfigurative images

## Decisions

Treat form, color, line, density, rhythm, and surface as sufficient content. Do not require a hidden landscape, person, story, or symbolic explanation. Determine whether the work pursues balance, friction, stillness, movement, interruption, optical play, or a different formal concern.

Choose geometric, gestural, organic, systemic, material-led, or mixed construction only as needed. The user may want sparse relations or an all-over field; one hero object is not compulsory.

## Visible description

Describe relationships rather than loose adjectives: a small dense cluster against a broad quiet field, near-touching planes, unequal intervals, an interrupted repetition, or a soft transition against a hard boundary. Color areas have relative weight and distance. Mark scale must relate to the canvas.

Define the role of support and medium. Exposed paper can be active space; opaque paint can establish mass; cut lines can create rhythm. Texture should contribute to the organizing relation rather than fill emptiness reflexively.

Preserve nonrepresentation explicitly when a phrase could accidentally summon objects. 'Tension' can be shown by spatial near-contact instead of a literal rope. Do not add stock symbols to explain an open-ended work.

## Conflicts and review

Check whether an added focal object, typography, perspective, highlight, or narrative would contradict the brief. Intentional irregularity need not be smoothed out. Minimal work needs precision of relationships, not minimal thought; rich work needs organization, not an inventory of effects.

Do not call empty areas unfinished or assign a story to validate the work. Review the whole field at reduced size as well as the surface logic in close view when an output exists.

## Ask or research when

Ask about a consequential geometric-versus-gestural or flat-versus-material choice when unresolved. Research a named artistic tradition only when specific fidelity matters. Otherwise keep discussion centered on the user's desired visual experience.
