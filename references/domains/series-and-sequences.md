# Series, grouped images, and sequences

## Decisions

Establish the number of outputs and whether they are separate images, one multi-panel composition, alternative concepts, or sequential edits. These are different deliverables. Identify shared identity/style rules and permitted changes. Ask about output form only if unclear and consequential.

## Visible description

Separate invariants from variables. Invariants may include character proportions, object geometry, palette roles, line quality, view family, or typography; variables may include setting, gesture, content, or scale. Repeat essential invariants in every standalone prompt without copying irrelevant detail.

For narrative panels, allocate a clear beat to each and define reading order. Control who and what persists, prop ownership, setting changes, and elapsed time where meaningful. Avoid contradictory directions between adjacent panels. Uniformity is not always coherence; deliberate shifts can mark a narrative turn.

For a nonnarrative series, use a common organizing principle with meaningful variation: crop, scale, rhythm, or subject. Do not change all attributes at once unless the series is explicitly about discontinuity.

## Conflicts and review

Check that every prompt is independently usable and stays under the character ceiling. Do not use 'same as above' as the only identity or style specification. References to actual supplied or accepted images remain useful, but name the image and intended attributes.

Check cross-image drift as well as individual quality. A series can fail even when each image is attractive. Preserve accepted appearances using available reference images while acknowledging that text alone cannot guarantee exact consistency.

For sequential edits, the next step depends on the previous output; make that dependency clear outside and inside the relevant instruction as appropriate. Do not claim a future input exists. A multi-panel output still has one image's space and readability budget.

## Ask or research when

Ask when output count, reading order, or what must remain stable is unclear. Research factual chronology if the sequence teaches a real process or history. Style alternatives requested for comparison should be separate complete concepts, not presented as one consistent story.
