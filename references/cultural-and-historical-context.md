# Cultural, historical, and craft grounding

Load when a named time, place, artistic tradition, or specialist technique makes accuracy material to the work. Ordinary creative briefs do not require a general art-history detour.

## Decide the fidelity contract

Distinguish faithful reconstruction, informed interpretation, and explicit hybrid invention. Do not turn an imaginative request into a reconstruction exercise, or describe a hybrid as historically exact. Ask only when the distinction materially changes the design.

Identify which claims need evidence: garment construction, architectural system, iconography, materials, object use, script, or production method. Research these specific unknowns rather than compiling an exhaustive chronology.

## Sources and translation into visuals

Prefer museum collection records, conservation accounts, primary artifacts, institutional archives, and documented craft sources. Read the source rather than relying on a search snippet. Attribute factual guidance outside the generation prompt; the prompt needs the resulting visible requirements, not a bibliography.

Translate knowledge into shape, proportion, joinery, surface, arrangement, and use. Avoid treating a national or regional name as a bag of decorative symbols. Do not invent sacred meaning, exact historical dates, or cultural provenance from appearance alone.

When sources disagree, state uncertainty and narrow the claim. If access is unavailable, offer a clearly described interpretation rather than pretending it is verified. Do not require research for purely formal choices that the user has deliberately left open.

## Review

Check for unintended mixing of periods or traditions, decorative symbols detached from the requested context, modern construction described as historical, and visual cliches replacing actual material detail. Preserve intentional hybridization after confirmation. No fixed preference for historical purity overrides an explicitly imaginative brief.
