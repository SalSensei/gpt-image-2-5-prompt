# Art direction across subjects

Use for concept development, formal resolution, and critique. These are decision criteria, not terms to insert into every prompt.

## Intent and distinctiveness

Find the work's particular observation, relationship, sensation, argument, contradiction, or formal experiment. Commercial recognition may take priority over emotional ambiguity; abstraction may need no verbal message. Ask what should be encountered first and what should remain after looking.

Resolve broad labels through choices. Luxury could emerge from proportion and material restraint or ceremonial abundance. Present that consequential fork when open. Originality need not mean novelty for its own sake; conventions can serve the brief. Do not fabricate a story merely to make a simple visual seem meaningful.

## Attention, shape, and rhythm

Choose intended viewing behavior: immediate recognition, distributed contemplation, sequential reading, or all-over pattern. One focal point is useful but not universal. Allocate contrast, scale, sharpness, detail, and position accordingly. A small bright prop can overpower a large muted subject.

Inspect silhouettes and the spaces between elements. Check unintended tangencies, background lines intersecting important forms, and crops that misrepresent structure. Deliberate compression or collision may be the idea. Protect breathing room when useful without imposing a fixed empty-space percentage.

Build rhythm through intervals, repeated shapes, directional flow, density, and interruption. Give exceptions a purpose. Stacking rotation, asymmetry, grain, broken grids, and eccentric type does not automatically create sophisticated work.

## Value, color, and depth

Resolve broad light/dark masses and subject separation before elaborate color naming. Low contrast or silhouette may be intentional. A factual diagram and nocturne need different readability thresholds.

Assign color roles: field, structure, focal accent, transition, reflected light. Discuss area and saturation relationships instead of listing many hues. Color codes express intent but cannot guarantee calibrated reproduction.

Choose photographic recession, layered planes, shallow relief, flat graphic space, or deliberate perspective contradiction. Match depth to medium; do not impose lens blur on a flat print by default.

## Detail and edges

Allocate detail where meaning requires it. Fine stitch structure may not register in a distant full-body image. Offer a larger visible motif, different framing, or a separate detail image if appropriate; do not add panels without agreement.

Choose crisp, soft, broken, lost, and merged edges deliberately. Let smoothness or omission balance texture. Global 'every pore visible, everything razor-sharp' language can conflict with focus and scale. It is a specific treatment, not a quality guarantee.

## Emotion and deviation

Use posture, implied pace, distance, light, objects, and context. Quiet absence may carry emotion without theatrical expression. Preserve ambiguity when requested.

For impossibility or distortion, articulate the stable rule and chosen exception. A stretched figure can remain visually balanced; floating architecture may retain material coherence. Review the work against its internal language rather than compulsory realism.

## Detail admission and stopping

For each addition, check purpose, perceptibility, medium fit, compatibility, and economy. Remove or defer failed additions. Unusual user choices deserve interpretation, not automatic correction.

Read the prompt for the whole image. Can its organizing idea be understood without an adjective list? Do secondary decisions reinforce it? What was added only from habit? Stop when remaining alternatives are matters of taste rather than unresolved defects. Do not endlessly rewrite to pursue an unsupported perfection claim.
